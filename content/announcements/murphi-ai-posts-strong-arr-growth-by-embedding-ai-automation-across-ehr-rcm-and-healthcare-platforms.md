United States, January 20, 2026 — For many healthcare software companies, the question is no longer whether to adopt artificial intelligence, but how quickly they can do so without destabilizing their existing platforms. As competitive pressure mounts, long internal development cycles are becoming increasingly difficult to justify.

“Healthcare platforms are realizing they cannot afford to spend 18 to 24 months building AI infrastructure internally when providers expect modern automation much sooner,” said Guru Tadiparti, Founder and CEO of Murphi.ai. “By the time those internal efforts are complete, market expectations—and customer demands—have already shifted.”

That reality is shaping how healthcare technology vendors think about AI. Murphi.ai, a Durham-based healthcare automation company, has recorded approximately five-times growth in annual recurring revenue between 2024 and 2025, reflecting a broader movement among electronic health record (EHR) vendors and revenue cycle platforms and other healthcare platforms, toward embedded AI rather than standalone tools.

Across healthcare IT, platforms are increasingly choosing to integrate AI directly into the systems clinicians already rely on, rather than asking providers to adopt new applications. The shift is driven by a simple operational truth: adoption accelerates when AI feels native.

That sentiment has been echoed by healthcare technology researchers. In a 2024 analysis on AI deployment models published by the Office of the National Coordinator for Health Information Technology (ONC), the agency noted that AI tools integrated directly into certified health IT systems demonstrate higher utilization and lower workflow disruption than external add-ons.

(Source: ONC, “AI and Health IT Integration Considerations,” 2024)

Murphi.ai’s growth suggests many platforms are acting on that insight. The company provides white-labeled AI infrastructure that healthcare software vendors can integrate under their own branding, allowing them to launch AI-enabled documentation, coding, billing, and operational workflows without building large internal data science teams or managing complex compliance requirements.

Tadiparti describes speed to market as the defining advantage of the model.

“We enable healthcare platforms to become AI-enabled within weeks,” he said. “That allows them to move quickly, meet provider expectations, and avoid the cost and uncertainty of long internal AI programs.”

Once embedded, Murphi.ai’s infrastructure supports workflows such as ambient clinical documentation, structured assessment generation for post-acute care, medical coding assistance, claim scrubbing, denial analysis, and patient-responsible payment automation. To clinicians and administrators, these capabilities appear as native features within familiar systems.

The emphasis on seamless integration reflects a broader concern in healthcare: cognitive overload. A 2024 report from the American Medical Association found that clinicians are more likely to engage with automation tools that reduce task switching and documentation burden rather than introduce new interfaces. (Source: American Medical Association, “Augmented Intelligence in Health Care,” 2024)

Operating as embedded infrastructure places higher technical and regulatory demands on AI providers. Systems must deliver consistent accuracy, low latency, and reliability while complying with healthcare security and privacy standards. Errors that might be tolerated in pilot environments become unacceptable when AI operates inside live clinical and financial workflows.

Murphi.ai has built its platform to meet those production requirements, aligning with industry standards such as HIPAA, SOC 2, and ISO 27001. These controls are often invisible to end users, but they are essential to earning platform trust and sustaining adoption over time.

The company’s business model reinforces its infrastructure positioning. Murphi.ai operates on a usage-based pricing structure tied to transaction volumes processed through partner platforms. As partners expand adoption among their provider customers, Murphi.ai scales alongside them, without traditional enterprise sales cycles or large upfront license commitments.

This alignment resonates with broader enterprise software trends. A 2024 McKinsey report on enterprise AI adoption observed that companies increasingly prefer modular AI components that integrate into existing systems, citing faster deployment and lower execution risk compared to in-house builds.

(Source: McKinsey & Company, “The State of AI in 2024”)

Workforce implications remain a central topic in healthcare automation. While AI is often framed as a threat to administrative roles, healthcare organizations continue to face staffing shortages across both clinical and operational functions. In that context, automation is increasingly viewed as a way to expand capacity rather than reduce headcount.

“Technology only works if frontline users see immediate value,” Tadiparti noted. “If AI saves time, reduces rework, and simplifies documentation, adoption follows naturally.”

Regulatory oversight continues to evolve alongside adoption. Federal agencies are refining guidance on AI use in clinical and operational settings, particularly where algorithms influence revenue cycle decisions or clinical documentation. For embedded infrastructure providers, governance and transparency are becoming as important as model performance.

Looking ahead, Tadiparti views Murphi.ai’s recent growth as an early signal rather than a destination. “We’re still at the beginning of healthcare’s AI transition,” he said. “The platforms that succeed will be the ones that integrate automation deeply, responsibly, and in a way providers actually trust.”

Murphi.ai’s expansion beyond post-acute care and the rollout of MurphiConnect.ai as a partner-led orchestration layer point toward that ambition. Rather than positioning itself as another AI application, the company is aiming to become a foundational layer that healthcare platforms depend on quietly.

In an industry often characterized by bold claims and short-lived pilots, Murphi.ai’s trajectory suggests a more measured path forward—one where embedded infrastructure, not standalone tools, defines the next phase of healthcare workflow automation.
